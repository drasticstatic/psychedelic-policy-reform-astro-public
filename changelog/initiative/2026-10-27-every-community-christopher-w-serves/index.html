Every community Christopher W serves, on one page

TL;DR: A new Communities page has a card for each community Christopher W serves: PIR®, The Holy Earth Foundation, ORG, Psanctuary, Psanctuary Church, and this initiative. Each shows that community's own picture and words, and opens its home on GitHub. The Community link leads there now, and the Facebook groups have a section of their own on it.
Read the full story

🌱 What changed

Christopher asked for one place that shows every community he serves, like the map: a card with a picture for each, opening not the community’s website but its home on GitHub, where its own welcome greets visitors first.

So the page has six cards. Each one carries:

  • The community’s own share picture, the one its link shows when shared, loaded from its own site.
  • One line in its own words, quoted from its GitHub profile (this initiative’s is its motto).
  • Open on GitHub first, and its website beside it.

Under the cards is the line these pages always carry: naming a community describes where Christopher W serves; it doesn’t mean any of them has endorsed this initiative, and nothing here speaks for them.

🛤️ The path this work takes

The page says it plainly: this initiative serves through sincere religious freedom, cultural history and community health. Others already carry the decentralization, medical and lobbying efforts, and every one of them helps. This path walks alongside them, not in competition with them.

💬 The Facebook groups, kept

The Community window used to list the Facebook groups. Everything it held is on the new page now, in the same words: the two groups Christopher W manages, the two related NORML groups (labelled related, not partners), the email updates still to come, and the sneak peek at a supporters’ network of our own. The Community link in the menus, the welcome guide’s button and the dApp preview’s Community links all lead to the page, and so does any old link to the window.

🌎 In Spanish too

The page’s own words switch to Spanish with the rest of the site (AI-written, and marked “translation in review” until a native speaker reads them). The communities’ names and their own quoted words stay as they wrote them.

Shared in Telegram, iMessage or Discord, it arrives with its own preview card.
