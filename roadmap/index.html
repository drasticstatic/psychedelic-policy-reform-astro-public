✨ Where it started

The spark was Kevin M’s “Roadmap to Reform” masterclass: a program on changing psychedelic laws through real-world policy work, from the advocate behind Decriminalize Denver, the 2019 campaign that made Denver the first US city to decriminalize psilocybin mushrooms. It inspired Christopher W to bring this work home to Pennsylvania. Naming the masterclass credits an inspiration; it doesn’t mean Kevin M has endorsed this initiative or speaks for it.

📚 An evidence library

The heart of the work: sources anyone can check, explained plainly.

  • Shelves: law and statutes; court decisions; research on safety and outcomes; religious and spiritual practice; policy across the states; and Pennsylvania. They’re already laid out on the dApp preview’s Evidence page, marked “coming soon”.
  • Every source gets a full citation, a link to the original, the date it was checked, and a short summary in plain words. Summaries say what a source found, not more.
  • Reviewed before it’s final. Explainers stay marked as drafts until someone has checked them against the sources. Corrections are welcome and are noted in the changelog.
  • Public sources only. Nothing private, and no one’s personal story or legal history.

🏛️ A Pennsylvania lane

This initiative is seen from Pennsylvania, so the state gets a lane of its own.

  • How the law applies here. The federal RFRA binds the federal government; a state’s own law governs the state. Pennsylvania has its own religious-freedom statute, the Religious Freedom Protection Act of 2002. A plain-language comparison of the two is the lane’s first task, reviewed before it’s published.
  • Following along. Relevant bills, hearings and public decisions in Pennsylvania, noted in the changelog as they happen, with links to the public record.
  • A summary page of where things stand in Pennsylvania, kept current.

🌱 The four communities at its heart

This initiative is meant to serve communities whose sincere spiritual practice includes sacraments the law still restricts. Four are at its heart: PIR®, Psanctuary, ORG and The Holy Earth Foundation. Each is a life-changing healing community, and this initiative wants to see their work protected, and blessing Pennsylvania. Each is described here in its own public words, with what this lane can offer it:

Community In its own words What this lane can offer
Psychedelics In Recovery™ (PIR®) “a 12-step fellowship of people from all 12-step programs and other paths of recovery” Plain, sourced explainers members can read for themselves, so conversations about the law start from facts rather than rumor.
Psanctuary “an inter-tradition church built on growing together with a sincere sacred mushroom practice rooted in spiritual autonomy” A clear picture of how religious-freedom law has treated sincere sacramental practice, to inform the church’s own decisions alongside its own counsel.
ORG (Octagon Religious-Research Group) “Where science and spirituality meet.” (its motto) An evidence library it can draw from and contribute to, on both the research side and the religious-freedom side.
The Holy Earth Foundation Home to Plants & Miracles, a teaching that brings A Course in Miracles together with plant medicine traditions. A shared, sourced understanding of how the law treats plant-medicine traditions practiced as religion, and a place to follow changes as they come.

Naming a community here describes who this work hopes to help. It doesn’t mean that community has endorsed the initiative or taken a position on it, and nothing here speaks for any of them. PIR®, as a 12-step fellowship, keeps to its own traditions.

🤝 Stewardship and succession

So these pages outlive any one person (today drasticstatic, Christopher W, owns both repositories and will hold the tokens that publish them). Details on the sustainability page.

  • A home of the initiative’s own: an organization (or the first partner’s) that owns these repositories, with at least two owners
  • Publishing through org-owned automation (a GitHub App, or a bot account), not personal tokens
  • A token register: every token and secret, its owner and its expiry date
  • A renewal runbook, with a reminder a month before each expiry
  • Hand-over steps written down: adding an owner, transferring repositories and the wallet-connect project
  • Exports and backups of the repositories and anything outside GitHub (the Facebook groups’ admin roles included), on a schedule
  • Wallet connect: the initiative’s own Reown project (one setting: the dApp’s NEXT_PUBLIC_REOWN_PROJECT_ID), before anything is recorded

🌌 Later, and still being explored

  • Pledges that count. The dApp preview’s pledge is a preview: a wallet signature, no gas, nothing recorded. Recording pledges (with clear consent, and no personal details) comes only after the evidence library is underway.
  • Tokens of gratitude. Beyond a signed pledge, a deeper and optional commitment: a soulbound, non-transferable token of gratitude from Christopher W’s gratitude-token project. It would stay in the wallet it’s given to and couldn’t be sold, sent on or traded; it isn’t money and it isn’t a vote. A sneak peek is on the pledge page. No dates, and no contract yet.
  • A supporters’ network of our own: open protocols like Lens and Farcaster, paired with a DAO where supporters propose ideas and decide together. Privacy first. A direction, not a promise.
  • The initiative’s own website and address, once its partners are announced.
